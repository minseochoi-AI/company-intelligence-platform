# Market archetypes and proof burdens

Classification follows evidence, not aspiration.

| Archetype | Minimum proof | Main analysis | Mandatory falsifier |
|---|---|---|---|
| `competitive` | Named direct competitors serving the same buyer/problem | share/concentration, differentiation, switching, channel economics | No plausible wedge within founder constraints |
| `niche` | Narrow buyer/workflow underserved by direct or substitute offers | reachable accounts, painful workflow, willingness-to-pay, expansion ceiling | Segment too small, inaccessible, or easily bundled by incumbents |
| `unverified-white-space` | Repeated searches found no direct offer but substitutes or workflow remain | problem existence, buyer budget, reason for absence, regulatory/technical feasibility | No budget/problem, prohibited data use, or structural non-adoption explains the gap |

Never use `uncontested`, `blue-ocean`, or `greenfield` as an observed status. A hypothesis stays `unverified-white-space` until paid demand and repeated competitor checks establish a defensible niche.

Record direct competitors, adjacent competitors, substitutes, manual workarounds, “do nothing,” and a large-platform bundling threat separately. An empty direct-competitor list requires non-empty search evidence and substitutes.
