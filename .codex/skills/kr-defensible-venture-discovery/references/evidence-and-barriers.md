# Evidence, rights, and barrier ledger

For every source record: locator, publisher, observed date, content hash, coverage, update cadence, acquisition method, acquisition rights, analysis rights, product-use/redistribution rights, personal or sensitive fields, retention requirements, and expected refresh cost.

Useful source classes include Korean public-data APIs/files, official statistics and notices, procurement/award data, patent and publication metadata, field interviews, product documentation, and appropriately licensed benchmark datasets. Kaggle is a hosting channel, not a single license. Verify the license of each dataset. Model-generated text is an inference that must cite underlying evidence; it is not a substitute for market observations or product-use rights.

Evaluate barriers independently:

- `physical`: sensors, retrofit constraints, calibration, certification, installation, maintenance, field access;
- `algorithmic`: measurable lift over a baseline, reproducible evaluation, know-how or patentability review, data feedback loop;
- `workflow`: integration, audit trail, procurement, training, switching costs;
- `data`: lawful access, uniqueness, label cost, freshness, coverage, product-use rights;
- `distribution`: trusted channel or institutional partnership.

A barrier claim needs an acquisition path, cost/time estimate, verification experiment, and copyability risk. Copyright does not protect an abstract algorithm; record the actual protectability mechanism separately and seek professional advice for patent or regulatory conclusions.
